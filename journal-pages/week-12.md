## Final Refinement and Development

As the project is approaching completion, this week focused on the final refinement stage. At this point, my goal was not only to improve the visual and interactive aspects of the project, but also to carefully reflect on the feedback received during studio consultations and make final adjustments before submission.

After receiving feedback last week, I identified an important challenge: how to transform viewers from observers into participants. I wanted the project to have a greater impact on audiences rather than simply asking them to look at my personal data. To address this, I continued developing the interactive features introduced in the previous week, including the new p5.js component that allows users to engage with the Bento Box system themselves.

Since the interaction had already improved significantly, I decided to focus on making the content clearer and more accessible. My main goal was to help audiences understand the visual language of the project more easily. To achieve this, I created a visual guide using Figma that explains the meaning of the Bento Box elements, including shapes, colours, and health scores. This acts like a menu or legend, allowing viewers to understand the visualisation before exploring the data.

I also expanded the written content throughout the website. New explanatory text was added to describe how the original food diary was transformed into data and how the Bento Box system communicates patterns of health, food choices, and emotions. These additions help viewers who may not be familiar with data visualisation understand the project more easily.

Another important improvement was strengthening the narrative structure of the website. Instead of simply presenting data, the website now guides viewers through the process from data collection, to data translation, to visualisation, and finally to reflection. This creates a clearer story and helps audiences understand the reasoning behind my design decisions.

Finally, I created a food diary section to show the original data before it was transformed into the Bento Box visualisation. To maintain a consistent visual style across the website, I used AI image editing tools to refine the diary page and present it in a cleaner and more professional format. This helps connect the raw data with the final visual outcome while keeping the overall presentation visually consistent.

## Project Statement
**Inside The Bento Box**

 Inside The Bento Box is a data-driven visualisation project that explores the relationship between food, emotion, and everyday health. The project is based on self-collected food diary data, including meal choices, mood, and meal satisfaction. These personal records were transformed into a visual language using the structure of a Bento Box, where shapes, colours, and scores communicate patterns that may otherwise go unnoticed.

In this visualisation, different shapes represent food categories, including vegetables, meat, and fried or sweet foods. Colours are used to represent mood and meal satisfaction, while a health scoring system translates daily food choices into an overall indication of dietary balance. By turning ordinary meals into data, the project highlights how eating habits are influenced not only by nutrition but also by emotions, routines, and daily experiences.

The project responds to a future in which personal data is increasingly used to understand health and wellbeing. Rather than focusing on large datasets or wearable technologies, this work examines how small and personal forms of data can reveal meaningful insights about everyday life. It also questions how data is represented, interpreted, and simplified. By translating complex experiences into visual symbols, the project demonstrates both the possibilities and limitations of data visualisation.

Beyond presenting my own eating habits, the project aims to encourage audiences to reflect on their relationship with food. Through the interactive “Build Your Own Bento Box” feature, viewers become participants rather than observers, allowing them to explore how their own food choices may influence health outcomes. Ultimately, the project invites people to see food not only as nutrition, but also as a reflection of emotion, behaviour, and personal wellbeing.
